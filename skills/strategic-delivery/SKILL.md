# Strategic delivery

## Purpose

Avoid completing a technically sound local task that cannot establish the
business outcome the work is meant to serve. This skill is for research,
automation, new capabilities, and other multi-step work with a meaningful
decision boundary. It is not needed for a bounded edit, a direct factual
answer, or routine maintenance with explicit acceptance criteria.

## Before material execution

State the following in the active brief or decision record. Ask a human only
when an answer changes scope, authority, success, or the next decision.

1. **Decision and user:** who will decide what differently if this succeeds?
2. **Business success:** what observable result would change that decision?
   Include at least one concrete non-success example. Infrastructure,
   measurement repair, and an exploratory association are not automatically
   business success.
3. **Evidence path:** which source, field, measurement, or experiment can
   observe that result? Confirm that the proposed operation can actually emit
   the needed information. A structural schema cannot establish a topic-level
   finding; a masked identifier cannot establish identity.
4. **Alternatives:** name the most credible competing explanations or routes.
   Compare each route in its best reasonable configuration (schedule,
   placement, discounts, scale); keep a worst-case comparison only as a
   labelled reference, or the assumptions decide the answer.
   Do not call one source an independent reference standard until its author,
   timing, and information source have been verified.
5. **Validation and stop:** define the minimum validity check, the unused or
   confirmatory evidence required before operational use, and the condition
   under which the next operation must not start.

If the evidence path cannot answer the success question, stop before spending
data access, model calls, or implementation effort. Record the decision gap;
do not substitute a convenient proxy and silently continue.

## While executing

- Keep separate labels for **enabler**, **measurement learning**,
  **exploratory hypothesis**, **validated finding**, and **operational change**.
  Completion at one level never implies the next.
- When new evidence changes the available path, revisit the success contract
  before extending a run or proposing a successor. Do not infer authorization
  for a new source, external system, or data purpose.
- Prefer the smallest read-only check that can falsify a presumed source or
  measurement path. Bind any later data access or external operation to its
  own authority and output contract.

## Handoff

Report these separately:

- what was completed;
- which business-success condition it does and does not satisfy;
- evidence observed versus assumed;
- the single next decision required, if any.

Do not present a list of optional investigations as a request for approval.
Ask only for the decision that changes the permissible next action.
